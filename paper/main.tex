\title{Control-oriented identification of low-dimensional L--H regime\\
dynamics from public MAST data}
\author{Kyounghyun Lee}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Operating a tokamak in the high-confinement regime requires knowing, in real
time, which confinement state the plasma is in. We identify, from \nShots{}
public MAST discharges, a compact causal gray-box model: a phenomenological
flux-coordinate temperature-diffusion backbone whose edge transport
coefficient is modulated by a scalar regime coordinate governed by the
depressed cubic $\tau\dot\lat = \dr(\inr) + \beta\lat - \lat^{3}$, with the
topology parameter $\beta$ left free so that one- and three-equilibrium
dynamics compete on held-out evidence rather than by assumption. Inputs
enter causally in fixed physical units; the D$_\alpha$ emission that
conventionally diagnoses the transition never drives the dynamics. All
components are identified jointly by differentiating through a verified
implicit--explicit finite-volume integrator. On sessions held out until a
single final evaluation, the model attains a median per-discharge regime
AUC of $0.948$ (95\% CI $[0.84, 0.99]$). Three results are negative and, we
argue, more useful than the positive one. First, the data do not select a
latent topology: a model constrained to be monostable matches or beats the
free model on every regime endpoint across seeds, so the reproducibly
positive $\beta$ of the free fit is not evidence that these discharges
require folds or hysteresis. Second, removing the regime--transport coupling
entirely changes nothing measurable, so the mechanism the architecture was
built around is not identifiable from this dataset, and we withdraw the
interpretability claim. Third, discrimination is the only quantity that
transfers: profile error doubles on held-out sessions, event recall falls
threefold, and a probability calibration fitted on validation data makes
test calibration worse than none. We also document two data-pipeline defects
--- an inverted flux coordinate and a metric that was never the
reconstruction's --- that were invisible to every training and validation
metric and that, once corrected, reversed the study's original conclusion.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The transition of a magnetically confined plasma from low (L) to high (H)
confinement is one of the sharpest macroscopic bifurcation-like phenomena
in laboratory physics: above a heating-power threshold, edge turbulence is
suppressed within milliseconds, an edge transport barrier forms, and
global confinement improves substantially \citep{wagner1982}. ITER-class
devices plan to operate in H-mode, so a regime estimate --- and a
quantitative distance to the transition --- is an operational input to
plasma control, not only an object of physics interest.

From a dynamical-systems standpoint the phenomenology is suggestive but
under-determined. There is a power threshold; back-transitions are
reported at lower power than forward transitions, which suggests
hysteresis; and dithering phases near threshold suggest more complex local
dynamics \citep{connor2000}. Reduced models of the turbulence--shear-flow
interaction reproduce this behavior qualitatively \citep{kim2003}, but
they are rarely identified quantitatively against experimental data, and
apparent hysteresis under time-varying forcing can also be produced by
ordinary finite-rate lag or by omitted slow variables. Whether experimental
trajectories \emph{require} a multistable description is therefore an
empirical question.

The modeling landscape offers, at one end, local turbulence calculations
(high fidelity, far too expensive for control), and at the other, static
or sequence-model classifiers of diagnostic signals (fast, but acausal in
their preprocessing, opaque, and silent about margins and dynamics).
Between them sit integrated one-dimensional transport solvers ---
ASTRA, JETTO, TRANSP, the control-oriented RAPTOR \citep{felici2011}, and
recently TORAX \citep{citrin2024torax}, which implements finite-volume
transport in JAX with differentiable solves aimed at optimization and
control workflows. This work borrows from TORAX the conceptual position
--- differentiable, physics-structured transport as the backbone for
gradient-based workflows --- but is narrower and more empirical: we do not
attempt a complete core simulator; we identify \emph{edge regime dynamics
and transition margins} from experimental data, a component such
simulators currently lack.

The gap, then, is a model that is simultaneously
(i) causal and cheap enough for real-time use,
(ii) expressive enough to represent an abrupt, possibly hysteretic regime
change,
(iii) identifiable from the modest number of well-diagnosed discharges a
single device provides, and
(iv) interpretable through stability quantities (equilibria, folds,
margins) that a controller can consume.
Neural ordinary differential equations \citep{chen2018neuralode} and
universal differential equations \citep{rackauckas2020universal} provide
the identification machinery; what they do not provide by themselves is
structure, and structure is what makes the result interpretable and
falsifiable.

Our approach is a structured (gray-box) neural differential model with
three parts: a conservative-form temperature-diffusion backbone with a
regime-dependent edge transport coefficient; a scalar latent regime
coordinate governed by the \emph{depressed cubic} normal form, whose free
topology parameter lets the data choose between monostable and bistable
dynamics; and a learned observation head that links the latent state to
the D$_\alpha$ emission proxy used for weak supervision. The neural
components are confined to the residual source closure and the observation
map; the latent vector field itself is a four-parameter polynomial.

The research question is:
\emph{can a compact, causal, continuous-time gray-box model identified
from experimental MAST discharges jointly predict edge-temperature
evolution and regime transitions, while yielding interpretable and
statistically defensible stability margins suitable for control-oriented
use?}

Contributions, each tied to a results subsection:
\begin{enumerate}
\item experimental identification of regime-aware gray-box differential
      dynamics from public MAST data, with verified numerics
      (\S\ref{sec:res-verif}, \S\ref{sec:res-profiles});
\item an explicit, falsifiable comparison between monostable and bistable
      latent dynamics under identical conditions, with uncertainty
      (\S\ref{sec:res-modelcomp});
\item conversion of the fitted vector field into conditional static fold
      margins and equivalent-power thresholds with uncertainty
      (\S\ref{sec:res-folds});
\item a causal, streaming-compatible evaluation protocol for
      control-oriented regime assessment (\S\ref{sec:res-events},
      \S\ref{sec:res-runtime}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Physical and dynamical background}
\label{sec:physics}

\paragraph{Device and measurements.} MAST was a mega-ampere spherical
tokamak operated at Culham until 2013. The public MAST archive
\citep{mastopendata} provides, per discharge: Thomson-scattering electron
temperature and density profiles ($\sim$\SI{4}{ms} cadence, $\sim$130
radial points, reliably measured mainly in an outer annulus); summary
traces of neutral-beam injected power $P$, plasma current $I_p$, and
line/profile-averaged density at \SI{1}{ms} cadence; filtered D$_\alpha$
photodiode signals; and equilibrium reconstructions from which the
flux-surface geometry is derived. The radial coordinate $\rho \in [0,1]$ used throughout is
\emph{constructed}, not supplied: it is the square root of the poloidal
flux normalised between the magnetic axis and the last closed flux surface,
$\rho = \sqrt{(\psi - \psi_{\mathrm{axis}})/(\psi_{\mathrm{bnd}} -
\psi_{\mathrm{axis}})}$. The Level-2 field \texttt{psi} is documented in
the files as the poloidal flux per radian (units \texttt{Wb/rad}) on the
$(R, Z)$ grid, and is maximal on the magnetic axis in this convention ---
a fact that caused the error described in Appendix~\ref{app:eq-rho}. The
same construction is used in training, evaluation, and deployment.

\paragraph{The L--H transition.} Above a threshold in the power crossing
the plasma boundary, sheared flows suppress edge turbulence; the effective
edge heat diffusivity drops and a pedestal forms. The D$_\alpha$ emission
--- light from recycling neutrals --- collapses at the transition, making
it the standard event marker; in H-mode, edge-localized modes (ELMs)
appear as short D$_\alpha$ bursts. MAST studies report threshold
dependence not only on power but strongly on plasma shape and X-point
geometry \citep{meyer2011}, which bounds what any model driven by a few
scalar inputs can capture. Published thresholds are conventionally stated
in \emph{loss power} (heating minus stored-energy rate and losses); our
corpus provides injected NBI power, so thresholds derived here are
\emph{equivalent injected-power} values conditional on the corpus, not
universal physical thresholds.

\paragraph{A conditional reduction argument.} If, near the transition, the
high-dimensional edge dynamics possess one slow real mode while the
remaining modes keep a stable spectral gap, and if the external inputs
vary slowly relative to those fast modes, then a center-manifold reduction
motivates a scalar coordinate governed by a low-order polynomial vector
field near the bifurcation \citep{kuznetsov2004elements}. A generic
saddle-node is locally quadratic; the \emph{depressed cubic} used here is
the minimal polynomial family that can represent either one equilibrium
branch or a pair of folds with two stable branches, switching between the
two topologies through a single parameter. It is a testable model class
motivated by these local assumptions --- not a theorem about the plasma.

\paragraph{Static bistability versus dynamic memory.} A monostable
first-order system with relaxation time $\tau$ already produces lag and
loop area under time-varying forcing; a bistable system additionally
possesses quasi-static memory: two stable states coexist at fixed input
between the folds. When the drive crosses a fold at a finite rate the
transition occurs after a \emph{dynamic bifurcation delay}, so a
zero static margin marks the disappearance of the quasi-static low state,
not necessarily the instant the observed transition completes. The
evaluation in \S\ref{sec:res-modelcomp} is designed around exactly this
distinction.

\section{Data and cohort construction}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Source and access.} All data are from the UKAEA MAST open-data
archive (Level-2 datasets over anonymous object storage; catalogue and
session-log metadata via the accompanying API), accessed August 2026
\citep{mastopendata}. The data license requires acknowledgement of the
data providers; see the availability statement.

\paragraph{Shot selection.} The corpus combines
(i) a contiguous experimental session (27567--27587),
(ii) discharges whose session-log annotations describe clear transitions,
back-transitions, or dithering,
(iii) dedicated L--H threshold campaigns (25141--25156 and 25206--25214,
X-point-height threshold studies, including discharges analyzed in
\citealp{meyer2011}), and
(iv) the 29934--29961 campaign.
Selection criteria were fixed before model evaluation: required channels
present (Thomson profiles, NBI power, $I_p$, density, D$_\alpha$,
equilibrium), adequate pre- and post-transition observation, and passage
of the profile quality gates below. Appendix~D lists every considered
shot with its exclusion reason or final role.
% manifest generated into tables/manifest.tex

\paragraph{Quality screening.} Raw channels are frequently corrupted.
Per time slice, profiles need a minimum number of finite channels and
radial span; impulsive outliers are removed by a rolling
median-absolute-deviation test. Per radial channel we require a minimum
median level, bounded relative jumps and span, and minimum time coverage;
a discharge is admitted only if at least four stable edge channels
survive.

\paragraph{Grouped roles.} Shots are grouped into experimental sessions by
contiguity of shot number; whole sessions are assigned to training
(\nTrain{} shots), validation/model selection (\nVal{}), or a locked
final test set (\nTest{}) by a deterministic hash, so neighboring shots
sharing machine setup can never straddle a split boundary. The test
sessions were evaluated once, after all modeling choices were frozen.

\subsection{Weak regime labels and audit}
\label{sec:labels}

Labels are computed from the D$_\alpha$ lower envelope (rolling 15th
percentile, \SI{15}{ms}): within a gate requiring an established discharge
($|I_p|$ above 60\% of its 95th percentile, density above 20\%), the
envelope distribution is split by a variance-maximizing threshold with
guards against degenerate splits; candidate H segments must persist
$\geq$ \SI{20}{ms}, be preceded by an in-gate L reference, and step down
sharply at entry (\SI{25}{ms} contrast windows), which rejects slow
drifts. Multiple H segments per shot are allowed. Samples near switches
and outside the gate are excluded from scoring.

Labels are audited against the independent session-log annotations
retrieved from the catalogue for every shot (Appendix~D). Discharges are
assigned to three evidence cohorts: \emph{confirmed transitions} (label
and session log agree), \emph{confirmed negatives} (both support L
throughout), and \emph{ambiguous} (extractor and log disagree, or gas
puffing/ELM behavior makes the proxy unreliable). Only confirmed
negatives support the negative-control claim in
\S\ref{sec:res-events}; ambiguous cases are reported separately and are
never treated as ground truth.

\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{States, inputs, outputs, and causal structure}
\label{sec:model-io}

Table~\ref{tab:notation} lists the notation. The model state is the
interior temperature profile $\Te(\rho, t)$ plus the scalar regime
coordinate $\lat(t)$. The causal input vector is
$\inr = [\ctrlq; \ctxm]$ with $\ctrlq = [P]$ the commanded neutral-beam
power and $\ctxm = [|I_p|, \bar\nel]$ measured context (plasma current
magnitude and profile-averaged density); density is a plasma response, not
an actuator, and is labeled accordingly. Inputs enter in fixed physical
units (MW, MA, $10^{19}\,$m$^{-3}$); no per-shot statistics are used, so
the same actuator setting produces the same drive in every discharge and
in deployment. Additional measured boundary data consumed by the transport
backbone --- the initial profile, the measured density profile, the
equilibrium geometry, and the edge boundary trace --- are stated in
\S\ref{sec:model-pde}; only the \emph{latent regime ODE} is driven purely
by $\inr$.

\subsection{Transport backbone (phenomenological)}
\label{sec:model-pde}

The backbone is a \emph{phenomenological flux-coordinate
temperature-diffusion model}: it evolves $\Te$ directly,
\begin{equation}
\partial_t \Te
= \frac{1}{V'(\rho)}\, \partial_\rho\!\left( V'(\rho)\, \chie(\rho, \lat)\,
\partial_\rho \Te \right) + s_{\theta}(\rho, \Te, \ne, \inr),
\label{eq:pde}
\end{equation}
with $V' = dV/d\rho$ from the equilibrium reconstruction. This is not a
complete electron energy balance: density factors, heat capacity, and
coordinate-motion terms are absorbed into the effective coefficient
$\chie$ and the residual source $s_\theta$, and the physical
interpretation of $\chie$ is restricted accordingly. Boundary conditions:
zero flux at the axis; at the edge, the measured outermost reliable
temperature is imposed as a time-varying Dirichlet trace. The edge trace
is measured data and is stated as such: it is available online only at
the diagnostic cadence, and its influence is assessed in the ablations
(Appendix~F). The initial profile $\Te(\rho, t_0)$ is the first
QA-passing Thomson profile (interpolated where masked); the domain is
$[0,1]$ but supervision is restricted to measured samples in the reliable
outer annulus, so core transport is explicitly \emph{not} identified.

\subsection{Regime-dependent transport coefficient}
\label{sec:model-chi}

\begin{equation}
\chie(\rho, \bact) = \chi_{\mathrm{core}}
+ S_{\mathrm{edge}}(\rho)\,\big[(1-\bact)\,\chi_{\mathrm{edge,L}}
+ \bact\,\chi_{\mathrm{edge,H}} - \chi_{\mathrm{core}}\big],
\qquad
S_{\mathrm{edge}}(\rho) = \sigma\!\Big(\tfrac{\rho - \rho_\chi}{\ell_\chi}\Big),
\label{eq:chi}
\end{equation}
with $\chi_{\mathrm{core}} > 0$ and
$\chi_{\mathrm{edge,L}} > \chi_{\mathrm{edge,H}} > 0$ enforced by
construction (softplus and positive-gap parameterizations), and fixed
layer geometry $\rho_\chi = 0.85$, $\ell_\chi = 0.08$ (sensitivity in
Appendix~F). The barrier activation $\bact \in [0,1]$ is the only channel
through which the regime affects the profile: the residual source is
deliberately blind to $\lat$, so a pedestal can only be produced by edge
transport suppression --- an ablation-testable interpretability
constraint (\S\ref{sec:res-ablations}).

\subsection{Canonical latent regime dynamics}
\label{sec:model-latent}

\begin{equation}
\tau\,\dot\lat = \dr(\inr) + \beta\,\lat - \lat^{3},
\qquad
\dr(\inr) = \kappa_P P + \kappa_I |I_p| + \kappa_n \bar\nel + \kappa_0,
\label{eq:nf}
\end{equation}
with $\tau = \tau_{\min} + \mathrm{softplus}(\cdot)$
($\tau_{\min} = \SI{5}{ms}$, below the observation cadence),
$\kappa_P \geq 0$ (monotonicity in heating power), the remaining drive
coefficients unconstrained, and $\beta$ \emph{free}. A quadratic term is
omitted because it is coordinate-redundant: shifting $\lat$ removes it,
with the shift absorbed by the drive intercept and the observation
offsets (Appendix~A). The character of the fitted dynamics is decided by
$\beta$: for $\beta \leq 0$ the equilibrium map is monotone and there is
one equilibrium branch (with $\beta = 0$ a non-hyperbolic boundary case
exhibiting critical slowing); for $\beta > 0$ two folds exist at
\begin{equation}
\dr_{\mathrm{f}}^{\pm} = \pm 2 (\beta/3)^{3/2},
\qquad
\Delta_\dr = \dr_{\mathrm{f}}^{+} - \dr_{\mathrm{f}}^{-} = 4 (\beta/3)^{3/2},
\label{eq:folds}
\end{equation}
and the system is bistable for
$\dr \in (\dr_{\mathrm{f}}^{-}, \dr_{\mathrm{f}}^{+})$. The conditional
static margins are
\begin{equation}
\mLH(t) = \dr_{\mathrm{f}}^{+} - \dr(\inr(t)),
\qquad
\mHL(t) = \dr(\inr(t)) - \dr_{\mathrm{f}}^{-}.
\label{eq:margins}
\end{equation} An equivalent
injected-power margin $\mLH / \kappa_P$ is meaningful only with the other
covariates held fixed. Derivations, the potential representation
$V(\lat) = \lat^4/4 - \beta\lat^2/2 - \dr\lat$, linearized relaxation
rates, and the dynamic-delay caveat are collected in Appendix~A.

\subsection{Barrier activation, regime output, and observation map}
\label{sec:model-obs}

$\bact = \sigma(k_b \lat + b_b)$ with $k_b > 0$ closes the transport
coupling. We report the same quantity as the soft regime coordinate
$\pH$; whether it behaves as a calibrated probability is \emph{measured}
(reliability curve, Brier score, ECE on held-out shots,
\S\ref{sec:res-events}) rather than assumed. The D$_\alpha$ proxy is
predicted by a small learned head
$\hat\yD = \sigma(h_\psi(\bact, \inr, T_{\mathrm{e,edge}},
n_{\mathrm{e,edge}}))$; the measured $\yD$ appears only in the loss.
This removes direct dynamical leakage of the proxy into the latent state;
it does not remove the statistical dependence of the \emph{evaluation}
on D$_\alpha$-derived labels, which is why the label audit of
\S\ref{sec:labels} matters.

\subsection{Initialization and online interface}
\label{sec:model-init}

$\lat(t_0)$ is set to the lowest equilibrium of \eqref{eq:nf} at the
initial drive (all gated discharges begin in L; discharges beginning in H
are out of scope). No per-shot free initial condition is learned. In
deployment the same rule initializes the state at gate opening; the model
then runs open loop --- there is currently no observer correcting drift,
and we label the system an \emph{open-loop regime predictor} accordingly.

\section{Numerical method and identification}
\label{sec:numerics}

\subsection{Finite-volume semi-discretization}
Uniform grid $\rho_i = i/(N-1)$, $N = 65$; node-centered cells with faces
midway between nodes (the axis cell has half width). Face fluxes
$F_{i+1/2} = -V'_{i+1/2} \chi_{i+1/2} (T_{\mathrm{e},i+1} - T_{\mathrm{e},i})/\Delta\rho$;
interior updates by flux differences over cell volumes
$v_i = V'_i \Delta\rho$ ($v_0 = V'(\Delta\rho/4)\Delta\rho/2$); zero
axis-face flux; the Dirichlet edge value is eliminated into the
right-hand side. The resulting operator is tridiagonal. A defect found by
the verification suite --- face-averaged $V'$ used for cell volumes and a
full-width axis cell --- stalled spatial convergence and was corrected
(Appendix~C).

\subsection{Exact IMEX update and differentiability}
With $L(\lat_n)$ the diffusion operator at the frozen current latent state
and $\bm{b}_n$ the boundary contribution, each substep advances
\begin{align}
[I - \thIMEX\,\Delta t\, L(\lat_n)]\, \bm{T}_{n+1}
&= [I + (1-\thIMEX)\,\Delta t\, L(\lat_n)]\, \bm{T}_n
 + \Delta t\, \bm{s}_\theta(\bm{T}_n, \cdot)
 + \Delta t\, \bm{b}(\thIMEX; t_n, t_{n+1}),
\label{eq:imex-T}\\
\lat_{n+1} &= \lat_n + \Delta t\, F(\lat_n, \inr_n)/1,
\label{eq:imex-z}
\end{align}
i.e., $\thIMEX$-weighted implicit diffusion (Thomas solve), explicit
source and explicit-Euler latent update, inputs linearly interpolated
within each observation interval, five substeps per Thomson interval.
The scheme is first order in time for $\thIMEX \neq 1/2$; for the linear
test equation its amplification factor
$R(s) = [1 + (1-\thIMEX)s]/[1 - \thIMEX s]$ is A-stable for
$\thIMEX \geq 1/2$ with stiff-limit damping
$-(1-\thIMEX)/\thIMEX \approx -0.43$ at $\thIMEX = 0.7$ (damped, not
L-stable). Implicit treatment removes the explicit parabolic step
restriction $\Delta t \lesssim \Delta\rho^2/\chi$; it does not remove the
physical time-scale separation. Loop bounds are static, so reverse-mode
differentiation proceeds through the discrete solver (discrete adjoint by
autodiff; no continuous adjoint is used). Gradient correctness is
verified against central finite differences (Appendix~C).

\subsection{Loss definitions}
All task losses are averages of order-one quantities with fixed scales
($s_T = \SI{100}{eV}$, $s_s = \SI{1e4}{eV/s}$):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} =
\underbrace{\tfrac{1}{N_T}\textstyle\sum W_{ij}\,
\phi_1\!\big(\tfrac{\hat T_{\mathrm{e},ij} - T_{\mathrm{e},ij}}{s_T}\big)}_{\text{profile}}
+ \lambda_s \overline{\phi_1(s_\theta / s_s)}
+ \lambda_1 \overline{\lat^2} + \lambda_2 \overline{\dot\lat^2}
+ \lambda_r\, \overline{\mathrm{BCE}(\pH, \ell)}
+ \lambda_y\, \overline{(\hat\yD - \tilde\yD)^2},
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with $\phi_\delta(x) = \delta^2(\sqrt{1 + (x/\delta)^2} - 1)$ the
pseudo-Huber function, $W$ inverse-coverage weights over measured samples
in the reliable annulus, $\ell$ the weak labels scored on clean L/H
samples, and $\tilde\yD$ the range-normalized proxy (a training-target
normalization only; model inputs are never normalized per shot).
Weights: $\lambda_s = 0.1$, $\lambda_1 = \lambda_2 = 10^{-3}$,
$\lambda_r = 0.5$, $\lambda_y = 2$; sensitivity in Appendix~F.

\subsection{Optimization and model selection}
AdamW (peak $2\times10^{-4}$, cosine decay, global-norm clipping,
weight decay $3\times10^{-5}$), batches of 8 discharges sampled uniformly
from the training sessions, double precision, early stopping on the
grouped-validation loss (patience 8 evaluations at 50-step spacing,
relative threshold $10^{-3}$). The validation loss contains all terms of
\eqref{eq:loss}, so checkpoint selection reflects regime fidelity as well
as profile fit. Parameter counts: residual source network
\resNsource{} parameters; observation head \resNhead{}; latent vector
field and readout 8 scalars; transport coefficient 3 scalars.
% parameter counts are generated into tables/hyperparams.tex

\subsection{Numerical verification}
Appendix~C reports: analytic decay-mode error, grid refinement
($N = 17$--$129$), substep refinement (1--20 per interval), discrete flux
balance (machine-precision residual), positivity, and autodiff-vs-finite-
difference gradient checks (relative error $\leq 10^{-9}$). End-to-end
observed convergence is first order, consistent with the
$\thIMEX = 0.7$ splitting, with absolute profile errors two orders of
magnitude below the model--data misfit at the operating resolution.

\section{Evaluation design}
\label{sec:evaldesign}

Primary endpoints, fixed before the locked-test evaluation:
(i) held-out per-shot event AUC of $\pH$ against clean-label samples;
(ii) event detection precision/recall and matched timing error for

(iii) profile MAE in the reliable annulus (free-run rollout, no
measurement reset);
(iv) negative-control false-positive rate on \emph{confirmed} negatives;
(v) the free-vs-monostable comparison: grouped-validation loss and event
metrics of $\beta$-free vs $\beta \leq 0$ fits under otherwise identical
conditions, with seed spread and Laplace uncertainty on $\beta$.
Secondary: calibration (reliability curve, Brier, ECE), per-cohort
breakdowns, and runtime. Uncertainty: per-shot bootstrap for medians;
Laplace curvature and seed spread for low-dimensional parameters; both
approximations are stated as such.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What was established.} A four-parameter scalar vector field,
driven only by causally measurable actuators and plasma context and
supervised by weak labels, orders confinement states within unseen
discharges at AUC $0.948$. The identified relaxation time,
$\tau \approx \SI{30}{ms}$, matches the observed duration of the
transition, and the drive extension motivated by MAST threshold physics ---
a loss-power proxy and plasma shape in place of injected power alone ---
improved held-out trajectory loss and event recall by margins many times
the seed spread. The regime estimate costs microseconds to propagate and
requires no diagnostic that is unavailable in real time.

\paragraph{What was not.} The study set out to test whether the L--H
transition in these data behaves like a fold bifurcation of a
low-dimensional system. It does not answer that question, and we think the
inability is the honest finding rather than a failure of execution. A
constrained monostable latent, which cannot exhibit static multistability at
all, describes the same discharges at least as well on every regime
endpoint and is the only variant that reproduces any back-transition.
That last observation has a mechanical explanation
(\S\ref{sec:res-modelcomp}): a bistable latent, once on the upper branch,
requires the drive to fall below the lower fold to return, an excursion
these discharges rarely provide. Hysteresis is thus simultaneously the
attraction of the bistable description and the reason it fits the observed
H$\to$L events worse.

\paragraph{The coupling that did nothing.} Setting $\Delta\chi = 0$ leaves
every metric unchanged. The architecture's central conceit --- that the
regime coordinate acts on the profile through the edge diffusivity, making
the inferred transport coefficient physically interpretable --- is not
supported. We suspect a structural cause: the measured edge temperature is
supplied as a Dirichlet trace and supervision spans only
$\rho \approx 0.73$--$1.0$, so the boundary condition already prescribes
most of the variance in the scored region. Withholding that trace and
predicting it from the model state is the experiment that would settle it,
and it is the single most valuable next step for this line of work.

\paragraph{Generalisation.} The gap between validation and locked test is
large and one-directional except in AUC. We draw two lessons. Statistically,
twenty validation discharges are too few to fit even a two-parameter
calibration map that transfers: the fitted map was actively harmful on new
sessions. Methodologically, a study of this kind that reports only
cross-validated numbers would have overstated its performance on
calibration, event detection, and profile accuracy simultaneously.

\paragraph{On finding one's own errors.} Three defects in this work
(Appendix~\ref{app:history}) produced healthy training curves, plausible
validation losses, and publishable figures while being wrong; two changed
conclusions. None was caught by a machine-learning diagnostic. They were
caught by a manufactured-solution convergence study, by auditing the data
path line by line against the file contents, and by validating a derived
geometric quantity against a value the files already contained. We would
argue that any study identifying physical parameters from experimental data
through a differentiable solver should budget for all three before
interpreting a fitted coefficient, and should report the fitted coefficient
only alongside a constrained alternative.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Beyond those already stated: a single device and a
narrow operating space; a thin supervised annulus that leaves core transport
unidentified; labels and observation target derived from the same
diagnostic; injected rather than absorbed power; a quasi-static geometry;
and a scalar latent that cannot represent the oscillatory dithering seen
near threshold, for which a two-dimensional normal form with a Hopf sector
is the natural extension.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether a compact, causal, continuous-time gray-box model
identified from experimental MAST discharges can jointly predict edge
temperature evolution and confinement-regime transitions while yielding
interpretable and statistically defensible stability margins. The answer is
partial and worth stating precisely. Regime discrimination transfers to
unseen experimental sessions at AUC $0.948$, using only inputs available in
real time and a latent whose vector field has four parameters. Profile
prediction, event timing, and probability calibration do not transfer at
useful accuracy. The stability margins can be computed in closed form, but
we cannot claim they are physically meaningful, because a model forbidden
from having folds fits these data equally well and because the coupling
between the regime state and the transport coefficient is not identifiable
here.

The next step is not a larger model. It is to remove the measured edge
boundary condition so that the transport coupling has variance to explain,
to add X-point geometry to the drive, and to test whether the resulting
margins retain predictive content on a second device.

\section{Canonical form: derivations}
\label{app:canonical}

\paragraph{Removal of the quadratic term.} For
$\tau\dot\zeta = a + c_1\zeta + c_2\zeta^2 - \zeta^3$, substituting
$\zeta = \lat + c_2/3$ gives
$\tau\dot\lat = \alpha + \beta\lat - \lat^3$ with
$\beta = c_1 + c_2^2/3$ and
$\alpha = a + c_1 c_2/3 + 2c_2^3/27$. Because the drive has a free
intercept and the observation maps free offsets, $(a, c_1, c_2)$ and
$(\alpha, \beta, 0)$ parameterize identical model families; $c_2$ is not
an independently identifiable physical coefficient. All analysis uses the
depressed form.

\paragraph{Equilibria and stability.} At constant $\dr$, equilibria solve
$\dr = g(\lat) = \lat^3 - \beta\lat$; the linearized eigenvalue at an
equilibrium $\lat_*$ is $\lambda = (\beta - 3\lat_*^2)/\tau$: stable for
$\lambda < 0$. For $\beta < 0$, $g' > 0$ everywhere and a unique
hyperbolic equilibrium exists for every drive. For $\beta = 0$ the
equilibrium at $\dr = 0$ is non-hyperbolic (critical slowing). For
$\beta > 0$, folds sit at $\lat^{\mathrm{f}}_{\pm} = \pm\sqrt{\beta/3}$
with drive values \eqref{eq:folds}; between them, two stable outer
equilibria and one unstable middle equilibrium coexist.

\paragraph{Potential form.} $\tau\dot\lat = -\partial V/\partial\lat$ with
$V(\lat) = \lat^4/4 - \beta\lat^2/2 - \dr\lat$; wells merge and vanish at
the folds. $V$ is a Lyapunov function of the reduced model, not a plasma
free energy.

\paragraph{Dynamic delay.} Under a drive ramp $\dr(t)$ crossing
$\dr_{\mathrm{f}}^{+}$ at finite rate, the escape of $\lat$ from the
disappearing well occurs after a delay scaling like the classical
saddle-node passage time; therefore $\mLH = 0$ marks loss of the
quasi-static state, not the completion time of the observed transition.
