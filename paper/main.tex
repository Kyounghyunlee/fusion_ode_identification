\title{Data-driven identification of low-dimensional regime dynamics\\
for the L--H transition in a magnetically confined plasma}
\author{Kyounghyun Lee}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The transition between the low- and high-confinement regimes (L- and H-mode)
of a tokamak plasma is abrupt, strongly nonlinear, and central to the
operation of future fusion devices, yet real-time-capable models that
\emph{quantify} the regime and the proximity to transition are scarce. We
propose a hybrid reduced-order model in which a one-dimensional thermal
transport equation with a regime-dependent transport coefficient is coupled
to a scalar latent state governed by the full unfolding of the cubic normal
form, $\tau\,\dot\lat = \drive(\ctrl) + c_1\lat + c_2\lat^{2} - \lat^{3}$.
The coefficients are left free: whether the identified regime dynamics are
bistable (folds, hysteresis) or monostable (a sharp but memoryless
threshold) is decided by the data, not assumed. The drive $\drive$ depends
only on measured actuators in fixed physical units, so the model is causal
and directly usable in a real-time setting; the D$_\alpha$ emission that
conventionally diagnoses the transition never enters the dynamics and only
supervises a learned observation head. All components are identified
end-to-end from public MAST measurements by reverse-mode differentiation
through an implicit--explicit integrator. The identified model assigns a
calibrated regime probability $\pH(t)$ at every instant, locates transition
times to a few tens of milliseconds, classifies non-transitioning
discharges correctly, and --- when the identified dynamics are bistable ---
yields closed-form fold margins that measure the distance to forced and
spontaneous transitions in actuator units.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Magnetically confined plasmas exhibit two robustly distinct transport
regimes. Above a heating-power threshold, the turbulent transport in a
narrow layer at the plasma edge is abruptly suppressed and an edge
\emph{transport barrier} forms: density and temperature profiles steepen
into a pedestal, global confinement roughly doubles, and the edge light
emission from neutral-particle recycling drops sharply. This is the
L--H transition \citep{wagner1982}; the high-confinement state (H-mode) is
the reference operating scenario for ITER-class devices, so knowing
\emph{which regime the plasma is in} and \emph{how far it is from
switching} --- quantitatively, in real time --- is an operational
requirement, not a curiosity.

From a dynamical-systems standpoint the phenomenology is suggestive: the
transition is fast compared with the profile evolution, exhibits a power
threshold, and back-transitions (H$\to$L) are reported at lower power than
forward transitions, hinting at hysteresis
\citep{connor2000, scheffer2009earlywarning}. Reduced predator--prey-type
models of the interaction between turbulence, shear flows, and pressure
gradients reproduce such behavior qualitatively
\citep{kim2003}, but they are seldom identified quantitatively against
experimental data, and machine-learning classifiers that detect the
transition from diagnostics are typically acausal, black-box, and give no
distance-to-transition information.

This paper takes a middle route. We identify, from public experimental
data, a hybrid model with three interpretable parts: a conservative
one-dimensional transport backbone for the electron temperature; a scalar
latent regime state $\lat(t)$ whose dynamics are the \emph{full unfolding
of the cubic normal form}; and a learned observation head that links the
latent state to the measured emission proxy. Crucially, we do not impose
bistability. The normal-form coefficients are free, and the identified
vector field may come out bistable (in which case transitions are fold
crossings and hysteresis is intrinsic) or monostable (a sharp threshold
response without memory). The question ``is the L--H transition a
bifurcation?'' is thereby moved from an assumption to a measurable property
of the identified dynamics.

Contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a causal, real-time-capable reduced-order model of regime dynamics,
      driven exclusively by actuators in fixed physical units
      (\S\ref{sec:model});
\item an end-to-end identification pipeline --- data curation with strict
      quality gates, weak-label extraction, and differentiable
      implicit--explicit integration --- applied to 60+ public MAST
      discharges (\S\ref{sec:data}, \S\ref{sec:ident});
\item a quantitative regime-assessment methodology: calibrated regime
      probability, per-discharge classification metrics, transition-time
      error, and, when the identified dynamics are bistable, closed-form
      fold margins in actuator units (\S\ref{sec:assess});
\item empirical results on MAST, including the identified normal-form
      coefficients and the answer the data gives to the bistability
      question (\S\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Physical background and how it shapes the model}
\label{sec:physics}

\paragraph{The system.} A tokamak confines a hot plasma on nested toroidal
magnetic surfaces. Transport of heat across those surfaces (radially,
coordinate $\rho \in [0,1]$) is dominated by turbulence and is orders of
magnitude faster than collisional theory predicts. The experiment measures
radial profiles of electron temperature and density at discrete times
(Thomson scattering), together with dense time series of the actuators ---
injected neutral-beam heating power $P$, plasma current $I_p$, and fueling
(reflected in the mean density $\bar n_e$) --- and of the D$_\alpha$ line
emission produced by recycling neutrals at the edge.

\paragraph{The L--H transition.} When the heating power crosses a
threshold, sheared plasma flows at the edge suppress the local turbulence;
the effective thermal diffusivity in a thin edge layer drops, and a steep
pedestal grows there. The D$_\alpha$ emission collapses at the transition
--- less turbulent transport means less particle exhaust and less
recycling light --- which makes it the standard experimental transition
indicator. In H-mode, quasi-periodic edge instabilities
(edge-localized modes, ELMs) appear as short D$_\alpha$ bursts on top of
the low quiescent level. The physical picture that emerges --- a fast edge
subsystem switching between two states under slow actuator forcing --- is
exactly the setting in which center-manifold arguments justify a
one-dimensional normal form for the switching degree of freedom
\citep{kuznetsov2004elements}.

\paragraph{How the physics enters the pipeline.} Each element of the
phenomenology is mapped to a model component:
(i) turbulence suppression in a thin edge layer $\to$ a transport
coefficient $\chi(\rho,\lat)$ whose \emph{edge} value decreases with the
barrier coordinate (Fig.~\ref{fig:chi});
(ii) fast switching under slow forcing $\to$ a scalar normal-form latent
driven by the actuators;
(iii) the D$_\alpha$ collapse $\to$ a learned observation map from the
latent state to the emission proxy, used for supervision only;
(iv) ELM bursts contaminating the proxy $\to$ label extraction from the
\emph{lower envelope} of the signal (\S\ref{sec:labels});
(v) the power threshold $\to$ the drive $\drive(\ctrl)$ is constrained
monotone in $P$ and uses fixed physical units, so a threshold in drive is
a threshold in watts.

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Source.} All data are from the public MAST archive
\citep{mastopendata}, accessed as Level-2 datasets over anonymous object
storage; discharge metadata and session-log annotations were queried
through the accompanying catalogue API. From each discharge we use:
the Thomson-scattering electron temperature and density profiles
($\sim$\SI{4}{ms} cadence, $\sim$130 spatial points, of which the reliably
measured region is an outer annulus $\rho \gtrsim 0.85$);
the summary actuator traces ($P$, $I_p$, $\bar n_e$) at \SI{1}{ms}
cadence; the D$_\alpha$ photodiode traces; and the equilibrium
reconstruction, from which the flux-surface volume factor $V'(\rho)$ in
the transport operator is computed.

\paragraph{Corpus.} The corpus contains \nShots{} discharges from the
2011--2013 campaigns: a contiguous experimental session (27567--27587)
plus discharges selected from the catalogue whose session-log annotations
describe clear transitions, back-transitions, or dithering phases.
\nTrans{} discharges contain at least one labeled H segment; \nNoTrans{}
--- including purely ohmically heated (beam-free) ones --- never leave L
and are retained deliberately as negative examples. Discharges whose
profile data fail the quality gates below are excluded entirely.

\paragraph{Quality screening.} Raw channels are frequently corrupted, and
admitting them destabilizes both the rollout and the labels. Per
time-slice, profiles need a minimum number of finite channels and radial
span, and impulsive outliers are removed by a rolling median-absolute-
deviation test. Per radial channel, we require a minimum median level
(\SI{50}{eV}), bounded relative jumps and span, and minimum time coverage;
a discharge is admitted only if at least four stable edge channels
survive. The representative density is a trimmed mean over the trusted
outer annulus.

\subsection{Weak regime labels}
\label{sec:labels}

Supervision for the regime state comes from segment labels computed
automatically from the D$_\alpha$ trace (Fig.~\ref{fig:labeler}); they are
deliberately conservative and enter the loss only through a low-weight
classification term.

Because ELM bursts are short positive spikes, the transition signature
lives in the \emph{lower envelope} of the signal: we track a rolling 15th
percentile over \SI{15}{ms} windows. Within a gate that requires an
established discharge (current above 60\% of its flat-top value, density
above 20\% of its peak --- excluding the ramp phases, where low emission
reflects low recycling rather than confinement), the envelope distribution
is split by a variance-maximizing (Otsu) threshold, guarded against
degenerate splits. A candidate H segment is accepted only if it
(i) persists $\geq$ \SI{20}{ms} (no label flicker),
(ii) is preceded by an L reference inside the gate (no ``H at gate
opening''), and
(iii) steps down sharply across its entry (mean drop $\geq 0.10$ of the
normalized range between \SI{25}{ms} windows), which rejects slow drifts
on density-ramp discharges. Multiple H segments per discharge are allowed,
so back-transitions are labeled. Samples near each switch are labeled as a
transition window and excluded from classification scoring, as are all
samples outside the gate. Where the session log gives an annotated
transition time, the labels agree to $\sim$\SI{20}{ms}.

\subsection{Transport backbone}
\label{sec:backbone}

The electron temperature $\state(\rho,t)$ on the normalized flux radius
$\rho \in [0,1]$ obeys a conservative diffusion law with a learned
residual source,
\begin{equation}
\partial_t \state
= \frac{1}{V'(\rho)} \, \partial_\rho\!\left( V'(\rho)\, \chi(\rho, \lat)\,
\partial_\rho \state \right) + s_{\theta}\!\left(\rho, \state, n, \ctrl, \lat\right),
\label{eq:pde}
\end{equation}
with symmetry (zero-flux) condition at the axis $\rho = 0$ and the
measured edge value imposed as a Dirichlet trace at $\rho = 1$. The
geometric weight $V'$ comes from the equilibrium reconstruction; the
density profile $n$ is measured. The residual source $s_\theta$ is a small
tanh network (four hidden layers of 128, zero-initialized output) that
absorbs heating deposition, radiation, and closure errors; a magnitude
penalty keeps it subordinate to the conservative operator.

Discretely, \eqref{eq:pde} is a finite-volume scheme on a uniform grid
($N = 65$): face fluxes $F_{i+1/2} = -V'_{i+1/2}\chi_{i+1/2}
(\state_{i+1}-\state_i)/\Delta\rho$, interior updates by flux differences,
axis handled by a zero left face flux and the edge by eliminating the
boundary node into the right-hand side. The implicit operator is
tridiagonal and solved by the Thomas algorithm.

\paragraph{Regime coupling.} The transport coefficient is a monotone
edge-layer profile whose edge value is suppressed by the barrier
coordinate $\pH \in [0,1]$ (defined in \S\ref{sec:latent}):
\begin{equation}
\chi(\rho, \lat) = \chi_0 + \sigma\!\left(\tfrac{\rho - \rho_c}{w}\right)
\left[\chi_1 - \Delta\chi\,\pH(\lat) - \chi_0\right],
\label{eq:chi}
\end{equation}
with $\rho_c = 0.85$, $w = 0.08$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:chi}). High $\pH$
suppresses edge transport and steepens the stationary edge profile ---
the pedestal --- so the latent state is identified not only from the
emission proxy but also through its effect on the measured temperature
profiles.

\subsection{Latent regime dynamics: the unfolded cubic normal form}
\label{sec:latent}

The regime coordinate evolves according to
\begin{equation}
\tau\,\dot\lat = \drive(\ctrl) + c_1\,\lat + c_2\,\lat^{2} - \lat^{3},
\label{eq:nf}
\end{equation}
the full unfolding of the cubic normal form: every structurally stable
scalar vector field with at most three equilibria can be brought to this
form, so \eqref{eq:nf} is not a bistability assumption but the most
general low-dimensional ansatz consistent with a fast switching degree of
freedom. The character of the identified dynamics is decided by the data
through the discriminant $\Delta = c_2^2 + 3c_1$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Delta > 0$: the equilibrium drive $g(\lat) = \lat^3 - c_2\lat^2 -
      c_1\lat$ is non-monotone; for
      $\drive \in (\drive_{\mathrm{f}}^{-},\, \drive_{\mathrm{f}}^{+})$
      (the fold values, the local extrema of $g$) the system is bistable,
      transitions are saddle-node events, and hysteresis is intrinsic;
\item $\Delta \leq 0$: $g$ is monotone, there is a unique equilibrium for
      every drive, and the transition is a fast but memoryless threshold
      response.
\end{itemize}
The drive is an affine map of the physically scaled actuators,
$\drive(\ctrl) = \kappa_P P + \kappa_I I_p + \kappa_n \bar n_e +
\kappa_0$ with $\kappa_P \geq 0$ (monotonicity in heating power) and $P$,
$I_p$, $\bar n_e$ in MW, MA, and $10^{19}\,$m$^{-3}$. Fixed units are
essential: with per-discharge statistical normalization, the same beam
power maps to different drive values in different discharges and no
shared threshold can exist. They also make the model causal --- no
whole-discharge statistics enter --- which is what permits real-time use.

\subsection{Observation model and regime probability}
\label{sec:obs}

The calibrated regime probability doubles as the barrier coordinate:
\begin{equation}
\pH(t) = \sigma\!\big(k\,\lat(t) + k_0\big),
\label{eq:ph}
\end{equation}
with learned gain and offset. The emission proxy is predicted by a small
learned head,
$\hat{\emiss} = \sigma\big(h_{\psi}(\pH, \ctrl, \state_{\mathrm{edge}},
n_{\mathrm{edge}})\big)$
(one hidden layer of 16). The measured $\emiss$ appears only in the loss
term for $\hat\emiss$: it never enters \eqref{eq:nf}. This breaks the
circularity of proxy-following formulations --- where the latent is pinned
to a transformation of $\emiss$ and ``classification from the latent'' is
just classification from the proxy --- and it makes the inferred regime
state falsifiable: the actuator-driven latent must \emph{predict} the
proxy collapse, and on discharges without transitions it must decline to.

\section{Identification}
\label{sec:ident}

\paragraph{Differentiable solver.} Diffusion makes \eqref{eq:pde} stiff
($|\lambda_{\max}| \sim \chi/\Delta\rho^2$), so we integrate with an
implicit--explicit $\theta$-method: the linear diffusion operator is
implicit ($\theta = 0.7$, tridiagonal Thomas solve), while the residual
source and the latent \eqref{eq:nf} advance explicitly. Five substeps are
taken per Thomson interval with static loop bounds, so the entire rollout
is reverse-mode differentiable under JIT compilation; integration is
frozen beyond each discharge's valid window so padding cannot destabilize
the solve.

\paragraph{Objective.} With $\Theta$ the full parameter set (source
network, $\chi$ parameters, normal-form coefficients, heads), the
per-discharge loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} =
\sum_{i,j} W_{ij}\, \phi_{\delta}\!\big(\hat\state(\rho_i, t_j) - \state_{ij}\big)
+ \lambda_s \textstyle\sum \phi_{\delta_s}(s_\theta)
+ \lambda_1 \overline{\lat^2} + \lambda_2 \overline{\dot\lat^2}
+ \lambda_r\, \mathrm{BCE}\big(\pH, \ell\big)
+ \lambda_y\, \overline{\big(\hat\emiss - \tilde\emiss\big)^2},
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_\delta$ is the pseudo-Huber function, $W$ inverse-coverage
weights that equalize sparsely and densely observed channels (only
measured samples in the trusted annulus are scored), $\ell$ the weak
segment labels scored on clean L/H samples only, and $\tilde\emiss$ the
range-normalized proxy. The profile term sits at $O(700)$ in these units;
$\lambda_r = \lambda_y = 20$ keeps the regime and observation terms at a
few percent of the total, which is required for the 14-parameter latent
to receive usable gradient against the $10^5$-parameter source network.

\paragraph{Training protocol.} 20\% of discharges are held out; the
validation loss includes the regime and observation terms, so checkpoint
selection favors regime fidelity as well as profile fit. AdamW
(peak learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$, cosine decay, gradient clipping,
weight decay $3\times10^{-5}$), batches of 8 discharges,
\resTrainSteps{} steps --- validation saturates well before this ---
with an exponential moving average of the weights tracked as an alternative
checkpoint. Training runs in double precision on a 14-core CPU in
\resTrainTime{}; a consumer GPU offers no advantage at this batch size
because the pipeline is float64 throughout.

\section{Quantitative regime assessment}
\label{sec:assess}

The identified model is assessed on four levels, all computed from
$\pH(t)$ and the normal-form quantities; none require manual inspection.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Classification.} Per discharge, $\pH$ is scored against the
      clean L/H label samples: accuracy and F1 at the $\pH = 0.5$
      threshold, threshold-free AUC (rank statistic), and the Brier score
      (calibration-sensitive). Transition windows and out-of-gate samples
      are excluded.
\item \textbf{Transition timing.} The model transition time is the first
      sustained upcrossing of $\pH = 0.5$; its error against the labeled
      transition time is reported per discharge.
\item \textbf{Negative control.} On discharges without a labeled H
      segment, $\pH$ must stay below $0.5$ throughout --- a direct test
      that the actuator-driven latent does not invent transitions.
\item \textbf{Normal-form diagnostics.} The identified $(c_1, c_2)$ answer
      the bistability question via $\Delta = c_2^2 + 3c_1$. If $\Delta >
      0$, the folds $\drive_{\mathrm{f}}^{\pm}$ exist and the margins
      \begin{equation}
      \mu_{\mathrm{LH}}(t) = \drive_{\mathrm{f}}^{+} - \drive(\ctrl(t)),
      \qquad
      \mu_{\mathrm{HL}}(t) = \drive(\ctrl(t)) - \drive_{\mathrm{f}}^{-},
      \label{eq:margins}
      \end{equation}
      measure the instantaneous distance (in drive units, hence
      convertible to watts through $\kappa_P$) to the forced transition
      and to the loss of the H state. Basin membership relative to the
      saddle gives the sharp (non-probabilistic) regime assignment.
\end{enumerate}

\label{sec:results}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Toward real-time control.} Every model input is a causally
available measurement in fixed units, the latent update \eqref{eq:nf} is
a single scalar ODE step, and $\pH$ and the margins \eqref{eq:margins} are
closed-form --- the regime assessor runs in microseconds per step,
compatible with plasma-control-system rates. A regulator holding
$\mu_{\mathrm{HL}}$ positive with a prescribed margin maintains
high confinement with a quantified robustness budget; scheduling the drive
through $\drive_{\mathrm{f}}^{+}$ forces the transition at a chosen time.
If the identified dynamics are instead monostable, the same interface
degrades gracefully: the threshold remains, only the memory (hysteresis)
term disappears, and $\pH$ remains the calibrated regime estimate.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The segment labels are weak and derive from the
same proxy the observation head predicts (though never from the latent's
inputs); label quality caps the classification ceiling, and dithering
phases are labeled crudely. The supervised region of the profile is a
thin edge annulus, so core transport is unconstrained. A single scalar
latent cannot represent oscillatory transition precursors (dithering limit
cycles); a two-dimensional normal form with a Hopf sector is the natural
extension. The identified threshold is a property of this corpus (one
device, one current/density neighborhood); transfer requires a nonlinear
drive map, for which the affine form is the first term. Finally, the
range normalization of the supervision targets is per-discharge and hence
acausal; this affects training only, not deployment, but a fully causal
training pipeline would tighten the real-time claim.
